Skin Cancer

An overview of the three common skin cancers, what they look like, and which ones behave urgently. This page is background reading; the appointment to book is a skin examination.

Basal cell carcinoma is the most common and the least aggressive, growing locally over months to years. Squamous cell carcinoma grows faster and can spread if neglected. Melanoma is less common and is the one whose timing matters most, which is why a changing mole gets a same-week appointment.

The general rule is change: a lesion that is new, growing, changing colour, bleeding or failing to heal deserves a look. Symmetry, border, colour and diameter are useful teaching tools, but change over time is the signal that finds the most cancers.
