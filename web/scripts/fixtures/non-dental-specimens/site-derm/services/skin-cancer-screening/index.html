Skin Cancer Screening

A full skin examination takes about twenty minutes and covers the scalp, the spaces between the toes, and everywhere in between. Most of what we find is not cancer. The point of the appointment is to identify the small number of lesions that need a closer look and to give you a baseline against which future changes can be judged.

What the procedure involves

We use dermoscopy for every suspicious lesion, which lets us see structures below the surface that are invisible to the naked eye. For patients with many moles we photograph the skin so that the next examination is a comparison rather than a fresh assessment from memory.

Who it suits

If a lesion needs to come off, we usually biopsy it the same day rather than asking you to return. Pathology results take about a week and we telephone you with them whatever they show. Nobody at this practice learns a diagnosis from a letter.

Recovery and follow-up

How often you should be screened depends on your history. Someone with a previous melanoma, a family history or a great deal of sun exposure may need to be seen twice a year; someone with none of those may not need annual screening at all, and we will say so.

Individual results vary, and every treatment on this page carries risks and limitations that we discuss with you before you decide.
