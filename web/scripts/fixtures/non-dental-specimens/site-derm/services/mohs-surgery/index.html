Mohs Surgery

Mohs micrographic surgery removes skin cancer in stages, examining each layer under a microscope before deciding whether more tissue needs to come out. Because the margin is checked while you wait, it removes the cancer while sparing more healthy skin than a wide excision would.

What the procedure involves

It is the technique of choice for cancers on the face, ears, hands and lower legs, for cancers that have come back, and for tumours with indistinct borders. For a small trunk lesion a simple excision is often the better answer, and we will tell you when that is the case.

Who it suits

Plan for most of the day. The excision itself is quick; the waiting between stages while the tissue is processed is what takes the time. Bring something to read, bring a snack, and bring someone to drive you if the surgery is near your eye.

Recovery and follow-up

Reconstruction happens the same day once the margins are clear. Our surgeon performs the repair rather than referring it on, and we review the site at one week and again at three months.

Individual results vary, and every treatment on this page carries risks and limitations that we discuss with you before you decide.
