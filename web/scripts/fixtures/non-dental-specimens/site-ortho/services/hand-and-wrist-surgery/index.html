Hand and Wrist Surgery

The hand is the part of the body people notice the fastest when it stops working. We treat carpal tunnel syndrome, trigger finger, thumb base arthritis, ganglion cysts and fractures of the wrist and fingers, in both working adults and retired patients.

What the procedure involves

Carpal tunnel release is our most common hand operation. It is done under local anaesthetic with the patient awake, takes about fifteen minutes, and relieves the night-time numbness that brings most people in. Office workers are typically back at a keyboard within two weeks and manual workers within six.

Who it suits

Trigger finger and thumb base arthritis are often treated without surgery first, with a splint and an injection. When surgery is needed for thumb base arthritis it is a larger operation than patients expect, with a recovery measured in months rather than weeks, and we would rather say that at the first appointment than at the follow-up.

Recovery and follow-up

Hand therapy is part of the treatment rather than an optional extra. Our hand therapists see patients in the same building, and for fracture and tendon work the therapy programme starts within days of the operation.

Individual results vary, and every procedure on this page carries risks and limitations that we discuss with you before you decide.
