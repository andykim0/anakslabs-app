Joint Injections

An injection is a way of putting medication exactly where the problem is instead of asking the whole body to carry it. We use them for arthritic joints, inflamed tendons and bursae, both to reduce pain and, when the diagnosis is uncertain, to confirm which structure is responsible.

What the procedure involves

Corticosteroid injections reduce inflammation and typically give relief measured in weeks to months. Hyaluronic acid injections are used in some arthritic knees. Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from your own blood and used for selected tendon problems. We will tell you which of these has good evidence for your specific condition and which does not.

Who it suits

Nearly all of our injections are given under ultrasound guidance in the clinic. Guidance means the needle goes where it was intended to go, which matters most in the shoulder and the hip where landmarks alone are unreliable. The appointment takes about twenty minutes.

Recovery and follow-up

An injection is a way to make rehabilitation possible, not a substitute for it. We book the therapy appointment at the same time as the injection so the window of reduced pain is used for the strengthening work that changes the underlying problem.

Individual results vary, and every procedure on this page carries risks and limitations that we discuss with you before you decide.
