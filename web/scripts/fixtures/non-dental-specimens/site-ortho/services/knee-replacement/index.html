Knee Replacement

Knee replacement is an operation for a knee joint whose cartilage has worn away, usually from osteoarthritis. When the cartilage is gone, the bones move against each other, and the pain typically arrives first with stairs and long walks and later at rest. We recommend the operation only after bracing, therapy and injections have been tried and the joint still limits daily life.

What the procedure involves

During the procedure the worn surfaces of the thigh bone and shin bone are removed and replaced with a metal and plastic implant sized to your anatomy. The implant is positioned with the leg aligned so that load passes through the joint the way it did before the cartilage wore down. Most operations take about ninety minutes and use a spinal anaesthetic with sedation rather than general anaesthesia.

Who it suits

Recovery begins the same day. A physical therapist has most patients standing within a few hours and walking with a frame before discharge. The first six weeks are the working part of recovery: bending the knee, rebuilding the quadriceps, and walking further each week. Most people return to driving between four and six weeks and to low-impact activity by three months.

Recovery and follow-up

We follow every knee replacement for a year with scheduled reviews at six weeks, three months and twelve months. Bring the walking aid you are using to each visit so we can see how your gait is changing rather than asking you to describe it.

Individual results vary, and every procedure on this page carries risks and limitations that we discuss with you before you decide.
