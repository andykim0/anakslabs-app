ACL Reconstruction

The anterior cruciate ligament stabilises the knee against twisting. It tears most often in sport, when a planted foot turns while the body keeps moving. People usually describe a pop, immediate swelling, and a knee that feels as though it will give way on turning.

What the procedure involves

Reconstruction replaces the torn ligament with a graft, most commonly taken from the hamstring or the patellar tendon. The graft is passed through tunnels drilled in the thigh bone and shin bone and fixed at the same angle as the original ligament. It is keyhole surgery and a day case: patients go home the same afternoon.

Who it suits

The rehabilitation matters more than the operation. For the first two weeks the priority is swelling and full extension. Strength work follows, then running at around three months, then changing direction. Return to competitive sport is normally between nine and twelve months, and we test rather than guess: we compare the operated and non-operated leg before clearing anyone.

Recovery and follow-up

Younger athletes tear the ligament again more often than older patients, which is why we do not shorten the programme for people who feel ready early. The graft takes the same amount of time to mature regardless of how strong the leg feels.

Individual results vary, and every procedure on this page carries risks and limitations that we discuss with you before you decide.
