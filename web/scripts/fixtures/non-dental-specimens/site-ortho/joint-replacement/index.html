Joint Replacement

Joint replacement is an option when arthritis has worn a joint to the point that it dictates the shape of your day. This page describes how we decide the timing with you, and what the first year after a hip or knee replacement typically looks like.

Timing is a conversation, not a threshold. Radiographs correlate poorly with how much a joint bothers a particular person, so we ask about stairs, sleep and distance rather than reading a number off an image. A joint that stops you sleeping is a stronger argument than a joint that looks worse.

The first year has a shape. Weeks one to six are about movement and swelling. Months two and three are about strength and walking distance. Months four to twelve are quieter, and most of the remaining improvement happens without you noticing it happening.
